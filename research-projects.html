LOS SONIDOS DEL ENCIERRO

Publication about this project: Article ↗

An ongoing research project of a sound ethnography in a prison context.

Ethnographic fieldwork is generally understood as a mode of knowledge production that seeks to describe and interpret a particular cultural milieu in order to make it intelligible to those who do not belong to it (Guber, 2012). Building on this understanding, we employ sound ethnography as a specific methodological device for conducting socio-anthropological research on social modes of sounding and listening (Vedana, 2010; Martin & Fernández Trejo, 2017), situated within a broader field of sound socio-anthropology. This field is defined by the explicit and reflexive incorporation of listening practices and sonic phenomena into socio-anthropological inquiry (Granados, 2018; Domínguez Ruiz, 2019). Seeger (2015) similarly argues that the constitution of an ethnographic ear is a process that began alongside the birth of anthropology and has been consolidated through “the interaction between the ethnographer and community members, who jointly focus attention on sound.” Sound ethnography can be understood as a distinct mode of listening through which ethnographers attend “to the sensorial forms of social life, where sound constitutes an important source of sensory information about the forms and arrangements of collective life” (Carvalho da Rocha & Vedana, 2009, p. 42).

The notion of sound space relates to acoustemology, a term that fuses acoustics and epistemology to highlight how sound functions as a mode and habit of knowing (Feld, 2012). Feld conceptualizes acoustemology as the recognition of forms of sensibility—especially hearing and sound production—as fundamental ways of being and orienting oneself in physical, social, and cultural space. Sound is both a form of knowledge about the world (Granados, 2018) and an emotive force. By combining these notions, we may speak of a sound environment.

Sound symbol also operates through mechanisms of evocation, understood as the capacity to reconstruct—through listening and in experiential form—primordial scenes or significant moments from one’s life.

SOUND MAPS

Sonidos que habitan nuestro entorno: mapa sonoro

Website ↗

In this space for research and creation, we work with methodologies related to sound ethnography, acoustic ecology, and audiovision. Through practices such as soundwalking, attentive and deep listening, and the recording and editing of soundscapes, we seek to deepen the sensory and perceptual experience of sound, exploring both its individual and collective dimensions.

This is an ongoing research project performed by students, graduates, and faculty members of the University of San Martin. The sound research project at UNSAM’s Miguelete Campus was structured around three processes: listening as a tool for recognizing territories; learning techniques for direct recording and editing and creating meaningful soundscapes; and creating an interactive sound map open to the community. The project also develops tools that can be applied in other social and artistic contexts, expanding social studies of sound through science and creation.
