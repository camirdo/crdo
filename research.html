RESEARCH

My research explores sound as a social experience, with a particular interest in listening practices, sound identities and the ways in which people experience and give meaning to their sonic environments.

I am currently continuing my studies through the postgraduate diploma Diplomatura Superior en Enfoques cualitativos transdisciplinares para el estudio de lo social (IDES–UNTREF). Currently based in France, my research interests also include migration and sonic gentrification associated with tourism.

We understand the importance of sound in social and cultural research as an exploratory resource capable of expanding existing inquiries about the city, community and social environment more broadly, and about the relationships and meanings that individuals assign to sound in particular, insofar as these sonic experiences constitute a significant part of daily life. Also, paying attention to sound allows us to discover practices of power within the urban landscape: “Attending to aural aspects of social experience accounts for this by rendering the means by which power is translated between people and environment audible” (Herrity, 2024).

Herrity, K. (2024). Sound, order and survival in prison: The rhythms and routines of HMP Midtown (1st ed.). Bristol University Press. https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.9692685
